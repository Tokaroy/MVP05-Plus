# MVP05-Plus Beta 2.5

Mods, Game Tweaks, your own season schedules, and short seasons that finish on their own.

## Highlights
- **Mods** (the Launcher's new Mods tab): *Add a mod…* from a folder or from files (or a zip), give it a name, and each of its files goes in place of the game's own file of that path or name, found for you (when the game has a file in more than one place, you pick). The list shows each mod with a checkbox, its name, its file types and its size; right-click a mod to turn it on or off, rename it, open its folder, export it as a zip (to share it) or remove it. A mod can come with a `mod.ini` whose `title` names it. When two mods have the same file, the one added first wins, and the list says so. Your game files are never changed: mods sit in their own folder beside the game, and one that's off isn't used. The Game Tweaks work on top of them: a tweak that changes a file a mod replaces starts from the mod's version.
- **Create a schedule** (*Schedule → Create a schedule…*): a season of any even number of games from 12 to 162. Every team plays the same number of games, exactly half at home, in 2 to 4-game series. Interleague as in 2005, matchups you choose, a new matchup each dynasty year, or none (with interleague, 30 games or more). Natural rivals every year if you like, and the All-Star Game hosted by the AL, the NL, or each in turn. A different season for each of the three dynasty years, previewed team by team before you save it to your list.
- **Share a schedule** (*Schedule → Export…*): a schedule you created or imported (or one made for MVP05-Plus) as a small zip. *Import a schedule…* takes the zip as it is.
- **Dynasty goals fit the season:** goals that count games or stats (home and road wins, total wins, team home runs, pitchers' wins, players' home runs) are scaled to your season's length, and so is the win percentage goal (its target and the figure shown beside it). A 40/40 season counts at the same pace (about 11 home runs and 11 steals in 46 games). A 3-year goal of 100 home wins is 28 in a 46-game season.
- **Short seasons end on their own:** a season shorter than 162 games goes on to the playoffs by itself. The game counts the games in your schedule when it starts, so it works whichever schedule is installed and however it got there.
- **Drag and drop** in the Launcher: drag a mod's folder, zip or files onto the *Mods* tab, or a save onto the *Saves* tab or the *Import a save* window. (Windows doesn't allow drops onto a program run as administrator; the buttons still work.)
- **Payroll Budget** (*Tools* tab): your team's payroll budget, and in owner mode the owner's bank balance, changed in the save. In the game, F10's *Dynasty* tab does the same for the one that's loaded.
- **Unlock everything** (*Settings* tab): every stadium, team, player, jersey and Legend, for whoever signs in, without spending MVP points.
- **Stamina Changer** (in-game Tools menu, F10, Extras): set the stamina of the pitcher on the mound, or the other team's, in the game you're playing. Based on tywiggins' Stamina Changer.
- **EA's debug menu** (in-game Tools menu, F10, Extras): the developers' own menu is still in the game, and a button opens it: cameras, replays, rendering, fielding tests and more. It was never meant for players and can break a game in progress (being tested).
- **Game Tweaks**: changes to how the game plays, built into MVP05-Plus. All are off unless you pick them (MVP 05 Default).
  - *Dynasty adjustments* (Tools tab): **pitchers' rest** (slow down how fast pitchers get their stamina back, so a starter needs about four days like a five-man rotation and a busy bullpen tires), **injury length** (injured players out half as long, or up to twice as long), and how fast players **develop** and **age**, how often they **retire**, what players **ask for** in new contracts (free agents and your own), the chance your players **re-sign**, and more star-level **rookies** (EA's own data tables, and the game's asking-price code for contracts).
  - *Graphics…* (Settings tab): more detailed players away from the play (the game keeps six at full detail and ten at the next level, and draws umpires and far fielders with two simpler models: on by default: the first ten of those get the second-best model); day games in daylight at Fenway, Turner Field and the other parks EA lit as dusk (the lighting, and a day sky from one of EA's own day parks); **rain** at every outdoor park (the game has rain, but only at SBC Park and at Safeco Field at night: showers in about 2 games in 9 there; this gives every outdoor park the same, or rain in every game).
  - *Mods* tab, at the bottom: **memory** doubled from the Xbox's 64 MB to 128 MB, growing the game's heap from 41 MB to 97 MB, for mods that load more than the Xbox could hold.
- **Extras** (in-game Tools menu, F10): **Unlock everything** and **Add MVP points**, EA's own from their debug menu, for the profile you're signed in with.
- **Dynasty** (in-game Tools menu): set your **Owner Mode bank balance** and your team's **payroll budget**, and **hold your manager rating** so you keep your job.

## League News (new, first version)
- **League News** (the *League News* button at the bottom right of *Play*, for your newest dynasty or owner mode; or right-click any save in *Saves*): your league in your browser, an MVP Baseball site laid out like a baseball front page from 2006, with the MVP Baseball 2005 badge from your own disc (Team Sites list, scoreboard, your club, hot and cold streaks, tabbed standings, matchups, a navy news column, expanded standings). **Scores & recaps** (a tab a day, oldest to newest) writes up every game of the last seven game days: the line score (runs by inning, extra innings together before the last one, then R, H and E), what happened (walk-offs, extra innings, shutouts, one-hitters, no-hitters, slugfests), streaks made or snapped, how the series and the season series stand, and where both clubs are in their divisions afterwards; the day's biggest game leads as the Game of the Day, and the scoreboard's boxes jump to their recaps. **Team pages**: every club in *Team Sites* (or a club's name in the standings) opens its page: record, place, streak and splits, the last 10 games (linked to their recaps) and the next ones, the 25-man roster with each player's season line (AB, AVG, HR, RBI, R, SB; G, W-L, ERA, SV, IP, SO), and the club's news, injuries and transactions. **Player pages**: click a player anywhere (leaders, rosters, transactions, injuries, headlines) for his position, club (and farm club), number, bats and throws, age, his 2005 contract while it runs, a status box (an injury, his latest move), and season-by-season line from the save's player history (his real seasons, then the dynasty's, minor-league years with their clubs; this season highlighted, career totals), and the stories about him. Club names link to their pages everywhere (standings, matchups, scores, recaps, transactions, Your club). **Hitting and pitching leaders** (left column, a box each): the top ten in each league in batting average, home runs and RBI, and in wins, ERA, saves and strikeouts, read from the save as the game's own League Leaders screen shows them (at least 2.7 at-bats a team game for average, an inning every three team games for ERA); before a season's first game, last season's, made from the save. It has the latest scores, standings by division with games back, last 10 and streaks, the wild card races, tomorrow's best matchups, the transactions wire (newest first) and the injury report. The top stories are ranked by how big the players are (star power, salary, top prospects), how much the teams' races make it matter, what happened (trades, injuries, call-ups, no-hitters, marathons, routs, streaks, a new division leader) and how recent it is. Your team's latest news always makes the front page. Transactions read like a wire report ("The Texas Rangers acquired RHP Vicente Padilla, 3B Hank Blalock and 2B Edgardo Alfonzo from the Chicago White Sox for ...", farm teams with their big-league club and level). The injury report lists every hurt player with his team and how long he's out ("about 3 weeks, expected back around September 4", or for the rest of the season), big-league clubs first. Names are the save's own, dynasty-made players included; positions, star power and salaries come from your own game. Nothing in the save is changed. Each injury is named as the game names it ("out with a torn rotator cuff for the rest of the season"), suspensions too, and the teams' logos are on the scoreboard, the standings and the top story; both are read from your game. Not in it yet: awards and All-Star votes.

## Saves
- **More than four saves of a type** (*Saves*, *Your saves*): the game holds 4 saves of each type (dynasty, owner mode, rosters ...). Each save now has a checkbox, as mods do: ticked saves are in the game; untick one (or right-click, Deactivate) and the Launcher keeps it (in *save library* beside MVP05.exe) until you tick it again. With 4 of that type ticked already, it asks which one to untick to make room. Nothing in a save changes; if the game has a save of the same name, it can go back under another. League News, Export and Share work on unticked saves too, and deleting one backs it up first.
- Rosters, dynasties and owner modes go **both ways** between your Xbox saves and the PS2, GameCube and PC versions: *Saves → Import a save…* brings one in, *Export…* (or a right-click on a save) sends one out, built on a save of yours from that console.
- **Owner mode saves** move between consoles too: *Import a save…* takes an owner mode from the PS2, GameCube, PC or another Xbox, and *Export…* (or a right-click on it in *Your saves*) takes one to the GameCube, PS2 or PC. As with dynasties, each goes into an owner mode save of your own on the console it goes to. Being tested.
- **Export a dynasty to the GameCube, the PS2 or the PC** (*Saves → Your saves*, right-click a dynasty → *Export to…*): writes a .gci for Dolphin's Memory Card Manager, a .psu for a PS2 memory card, or a PC .sav, built on one of your own dynasty saves from there. The GameCube and the PS2 keep each player's last 6 seasons of history; the PC keeps all 16.
- **Export rosters to the GameCube, PS2 or PC** (*Saves*: pick a roster save, *Export…*, or right-click it): built on a roster save of yours from that console, as dynasties are.
- **GameCube dynasty imports** keep a traded player's older seasons that could be dropped before.
- **Exports straight onto a PS2 memory card:** choose your PCSX2 memory card (`.ps2`) to build on and you get a copy of the card with the save in place of the one it was built on (rosters, dynasty or owner mode), ready to put in a PCSX2 slot. Your card isn't changed. A `.psu` still gives a `.psu`.
- **Dolphin GameCube memory cards (.raw):** import a save straight from your Dolphin card (you pick the save if there's more than one), and export onto a copy of the card in place of the save it's built on. No Memory Card Manager steps. (Made-up cards only so far: tell us if your card isn't read.)
- **Import as a new save:** instead of replacing one of yours, pick *As a new save…* at the end of the list and name it. It's made in the folder the game itself would use for that name, so saving it in the game keeps one copy.
- **Copy, rename and delete saves** (*Saves*, right-click a save): a copy under a new name (to try something in a dynasty without losing it), the save renamed, or deleted (backed up first). A copy or a renamed save goes in the folder the game uses for the name.
- **PC and GameCube exports get a name of their own:** the new .sav is named and called inside like the PC game names its saves (RESOX2_Dynasty.sav is RESOX2 in the game), so saving it in the PC game doesn't write over the save it was built on. A GameCube .gci export is <name>2 too, so Dolphin puts it on the card beside the one it was built on (an export onto a Dolphin card still takes that save's place).
- **Saves in a zip or a folder:** *Import a save* (or a drop on the Saves tab) takes a zip or a folder as people share them, and finds the save in it (you pick one if there are several). *Export… → As a zip, to share* (or right-click a save) makes that zip from one of yours.
- **Import and export remember where you were:** the file pickers open in the folder you last chose from, or the first time in Dolphin's or PCSX2's own card folder.
- **Problems are written down:** a failed import or export, or anything unexpected in the Launcher, goes to `MVP05-Plus-Launcher.log` beside MVP05.exe, and the message says which part of the save it was.
- **The Launcher's backups show in Saves → Backups:** the copy of a save made before an import, a Payroll Budget or Playoffs change, a rename or a delete is listed by date ("before importing a dynasty" and so on) and restores from there. Restoring one of those puts back only that save; your other saves stay. (The ones the earlier Beta 2.5 builds made are in the *save backups* folder beside MVP05.exe, but not in the list.)
- **Standings Projector on a saved dynasty past the All-Star break:** the save keeps each team's record in two halves, split at the break. The Launcher's projector refused a save once the second half had started, which is when a short season ends. It now projects into both halves, as the in-game one does.
- **Played dynasties to and from the GameCube:** the converters find each team's roster by its 4 lineups in a row. In a played season, a team's data can end in bytes that look like one more lineup, and the converter counted 127 teams and stopped ("rosters didn't come out as expected"). Seen on a KC 2006 dynasty when exporting it to the GameCube; GameCube imports find the teams the same way, which may be what stopped the Stros import. Fixed in both directions, and that dynasty now goes to the GameCube and back exactly. A team's lineup unit is now found by its marker alone, so a lineup slot holding an unexpected value (an empty slot, say) no longer hides it; one hidden and one extra gave the "no ring chain" error when KCRTEST was exported to the GameCube.

## Changed
- **Standings Projector** (was the Short Season Advancer): no longer needed for short seasons. It's kept for a dynasty stuck at the end of a short season from an older version: in the game it's now a checkbox in F10's *Extras* tab.

## Known issues
- **Switching schedules mid-season:** a new schedule starts with the next season, and the season you're in is played out at its own length (the game reads it from the dynasty itself). One limit: if you switch during the playoffs, the new length is used at once, so switch before the playoffs or after them.

## Updating
From Beta 2 or 2.1: *Settings → Updates*. From Beta 1: unzip into a new folder and bring your old one over when the Launcher asks.

# MVP05-Plus Beta 2

Bring your saves over from the other versions of MVP 2005, pick the season you play, run tools inside the game, and update from the Launcher.

## Highlights
- **Mods** (the Launcher's new Mods tab): add a mod's zip, files or folder, and each of its files goes in place of the game's own file of that path or name, found for you (when the game has a file in more than one place, you pick). One checkbox per mod, however many files it has; put them in order, and when two mods have the same file the higher one wins (the list says which). Your game files are never changed: mods sit in their own folder beside the game, and one that's off isn't used. The Game Tweaks work on top of them: a tweak that changes a file a mod replaces starts from the mod's version.
- **Import saves from the PS2, GameCube, PC and Xbox** (*Saves → Import a save…*): rosters and dynasties, converted into one of your MVP05-Plus saves so they play here. Your save is backed up first.
- **Custom season schedules** (*Schedule* tab): the real 2005 season, or its 162 games shuffled each time you play. A 64-game season made for MVP05-Plus balances division, league and interleague games. Basic 60, 30 and 15-game schedules are included, or you can import your own.
- **Tools** (the Launcher's *Tools* tab, and in the game: F10, or hold Back + Start / Z + Start): a Short Season Advancer, a Team Changer and a Playoffs Editor.
- **Updates from the Launcher** (*Settings → Updates*): check, install and undo, from this version on.

## Import saves
*Saves → Your saves → Import a save…*: choose the save, pick which of your saves it goes into (it's backed up first, in *Saves → Backups*), and import. Save your rosters, or start a dynasty and save it, in the game once so you have a save of that kind to import into.
- **PS2:** rosters and dynasties, from a `.psu`, a PCSX2 memory card (`.ps2`: you pick the save on it), or the `.sav` from a save.
- **GameCube:** rosters and dynasties, from the `.gci` Dolphin exports.
- **PC:** rosters and dynasties (`.sav`).
- **Xbox:** rosters and dynasties from another Xbox (its `data.dat`).
- Owner mode conversions are new: the GameCube's has only been checked against a new owner mode, not a played one.

Roster conversion is **Mad Dog**'s, from his MVP Roster Toolkit (the same file it makes). GameCube conversion, rosters and dynasties, is **spy_jam**'s. PS2 and PC dynasties use both.

## Custom season schedules
The *Schedule* tab sets the schedule new seasons play. Only the three MLB schedules in your game's `schedule.big` change (the minor leagues and spring training stay the game's own), and your original is kept.
- **162 games:** the MVP 2005 disc's, the real 2005 season.
- **162 games, shuffled:** the 2005 schedule with the teams in each division dealt one another's seasons. Every team keeps 81 home games and the same division, league and interleague games, against opponents in a new order; dates and the All-Star break stay put. A new shuffle every time the Launcher starts the game; a season already under way keeps its own.
- **64 games,** made for MVP05-Plus: 6 games against each division rival, 2 or 3 against the rest of the league, every interleague team once (the natural rival twice), even home and away, the All-Star Game in July and a real trade deadline.
- **60, 30 and 15 games:** basic short seasons.
- **Import a schedule:** a `schedule.big`, or its `mlb162_1/2/3.dat` files, from any version of MVP 2005 (they're the same on every platform). It's checked (all 30 teams, the same number of games each), joins the list, can be removed again, and updates never touch it.

## Tools
In the game's Tools menu (F10, or hold Back + Start for a second, Z + Start on a GameCube controller; any controller or the mouse moves around it, and the game gets no input while it's open) and in the Launcher's *Tools* tab:
- **Short Season Advancer:** a season shorter than 162 games stops at the end of the regular season until the standings are 162 games long. At its end, it finds the standings and writes them projected to 162 games, every team keeping its winning percentage, so the season goes on to the playoffs. The season's length comes from the *Schedule* tab. In the Launcher it also works on a saved dynasty with the game closed.
- **Team Changer:** take over another organization in your dynasty or owner mode; save to keep it. A port of **tywiggins'** MVP 2005 Team Changer (2006, for the PC game).
- **Playoffs Editor:** choose who plays in the playoffs. When a playoff round has started, before its first game, swap any team in it for another organization; each series keeps its dates and home field. In the running game or on a saved dynasty. Based on **tywiggins'** MVP 2005 Playoffs Creator (2006, for the PC game).

tywiggins' tools were made for the PC game; the Xbox game keeps its database in memory the same way, so they carry over.

## Launcher
- **Updates** (*Settings → Updates*): shows your version, checks GitHub for a newer release (also when the Launcher opens, unless you turn that off), shows what's new and installs it: the program files only, checked against the release's manifest. Your game files, saves, backups, button art, shader cache and imported schedules are never touched, and your settings are kept, with new options added. *Back to …* undoes the last update.
- **A tidier layout:** *Play*, *Saves* (your saves and backups), *Schedule*, *Tools* (a list: open the one you want) and *Settings* (desktop shortcuts and updates side by side).
- **Your game's title art** on the *Play* tab, from your own Xbox disc.
- **Controllers:** an Xbox-style controller, or a GameCube controller on the adapter (with its WinUSB driver), moves around the Launcher: d-pad or stick, A presses, B goes back, LB / RB (L / R) switch tabs, Start launches the game from *Play*.
- **Bring over an older install:** the first time the Launcher opens in a new folder (or on Ctrl+Shift+B), it offers to bring MVP05-Plus Beta 1 or Mad Dog's MVP05 over: its game, saves and settings, copied or moved in.
- **Install progress:** one bar across the whole install ("Step 2 of 3: making the PS2 button art") with the time so far.
- *Launch MVP 05* minimizes the Launcher instead of closing it, so it's an alt-tab away while you play; it closes when the game does, unless you've brought it back up.

## Game
- **Low latency** (*Settings*, off by default): one finished frame waits for the screen instead of up to three, for up to about two frames (~33 ms at 60 Hz) less delay between a button and the screen. It can stutter on a slower PC, so it starts off.
- **Esc** asks "Quit MVP05-Plus?".

## Fixed
- The Launcher's *Settings* no longer drops the note after a text setting (such as `display_mode`) in `mvp05-settings.toml`.

## Coming from Beta 1 or Mad Dog's MVP05
Unzip MVP05-Plus into a new folder and open the Launcher. It asks to bring over your game, saves and settings, so you don't need your discs again. Said no? Press **Ctrl+Shift+B** in the Launcher to be asked again. From Beta 2 on, *Settings → Updates* updates MVP05-Plus for you.

# MVP05-Plus Beta 1

First release of MVP05-Plus by spy_jam, built on Mad Dog's MVP05 Public Beta 3 (made with TGP482's XboxXBERecomp).

## New
- **GameCube controllers** through a Wii U / Switch GameCube adapter (official or compatible, in Wii U mode), all 4 ports, with rumble. Stick, C-stick and triggers handled as the GameCube release does. Hold X + Y + Start for 3 seconds to recentre.
- **PlayStation controllers** with the PS2 release's stick handling, analog button pressure and rumble.
- **Native control layouts:** GameCube controllers use the GameCube release's layout, PlayStation controllers the PS2 release's.
- **Button art:** GameCube or PS2 button pictures in menus, help bars and in-game, picked from the controller connected at start. Built by the Launcher from your own discs (optional).
- **MVP05-Plus Launcher** (`MVP05-Plus-Launcher.exe`): installs from your discs (the Xbox game into `assets/`, and the button art from only the files it needs on your GameCube and PS2 discs), then opens on *Play*, with Launch and which controllers Windows sees, including whether the GameCube adapter has its WinUSB driver. Also *Saves* and *Settings* (display mode, aspect ratio, resolution and more, and desktop shortcuts if you want them). Portable: everything stays in the game folder.
- **Shader cache** in `shader_cache/`, so shaders compile once instead of every run.
- **The game's icon**, made from your own Xbox disc by the Launcher.
- **Save backups** at startup, in `save backups/` (30 kept by default), listed in the Launcher's *Saves* tab, where you can restore single saves or a whole date.
- **Esc quits** after asking (Alt+F4 still works). Don't quit while the game is saving.
- **Fullscreen by default**, at your desktop's own resolution (Mad Dog's release starts windowed at 1920 x 1080); change it in the Launcher's *Settings* tab.

## Fixed
- A crash during stolen bases, found in testing.
- SteamOS (through Proton): the black screen; the GameCube adapter through `gcbridge` (see the README for the one-time set-up).

## Known issues
- Under Proton, the GameCube adapter needs a one-time set-up (`./gcbridge --install`, see the README) or the Steam launch option; the game can't start `gcbridge` by itself.
- Display issues from the base recompilation (for example 720p fonts) are unchanged.

## Requirements
Windows 10/11 x64 (or SteamOS with Proton), a DirectX 12 graphics card, about 4 GB free, and your own MVP Baseball 2005 Xbox disc image. GameCube and PS2 discs are optional, for button art. Nothing else to install.
